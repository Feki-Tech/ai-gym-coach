# Recovery, soreness and warm-up

## Warm-up
5 minutes of easy cardio or bodyweight movement, then 2-3 ramp-up sets of
the first exercise (empty bar / 50% / 75% of the working weight, fewer
reps). Add mobility for the joints that limit depth: ankle rocks for
squats, hip openers for lunges, band pull-aparts for pressing.

## Soreness vs. pain
Dull, even muscle ache 24-72 h after training (DOMS) is normal, peaks on
day two and eases with light movement. Sharp, stabbing, radiating pain,
numbness, tingling, swelling or pain in a joint (not the muscle) is not
normal: stop the exercise and have it checked by a physio or doctor.

## Sleep
7-9 hours a night is the single biggest recovery lever. Under 6 hours
reduces strength, coordination and motivation the next day.

## Rest between sets, with heart rate
With a heart-rate sensor the app ends the rest when your heart rate has
recovered ~35% of the way from the set's peak toward your resting rate —
that is a better signal than a fixed timer. Without a sensor: 60-90 s for
accessories, 2-3 min for heavy compound lifts.

## Heart-rate zones
Zone 1 (50-60% of max) recovery, zone 2 (60-70%) easy aerobic base, zone 3
(70-80%) moderate, zone 4 (80-90%) hard, zone 5 (90-100%) maximal.
Strength sets spike into zone 3-4 and should recover to zone 1-2 before the
next set. Max heart rate estimated as 220 minus age is a rough formula; a
measured max is better.

## Fatigue management
Two or more sessions in a row with lower scores, slower reps and higher
resting heart rate = accumulated fatigue. Take an extra rest day or a
lighter session, sleep, eat enough.
